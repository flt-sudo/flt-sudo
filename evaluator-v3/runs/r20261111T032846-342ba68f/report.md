# Evaluation: Proposed $30M acquisition of Tamarind Field Services: whether the price fits the board's 7.0x bolt-on cap once the quality-of-earnings (QoE) adjustments are applied

**Decision objective:** Decide whether the deal committee can approve signing at $30M under its delegated authority, or whether it must renegotiate the price or structure, or take the deal back to the full board. The board mandate caps bolt-ons at 7.0x diligence-adjusted (QoE) EBITDA, not the banker's CIM adjusted EBITDA. The test is therefore: purchase price (on the basis the mandate intends) divided by QoE-adjusted EBITDA, compared against 7.0x. If the deal breaches the cap, work out the maximum compliant price and the renegotiation levers.

## Verdict

No. On the evidence supplied, the deal committee cannot approve signing at $30M under its delegated authority. The board mandate caps bolt-ons at 7.0x diligence-adjusted (QoE) EBITDA, not the banker's CIM adjusted EBITDA (E1). QoE EBITDA is $3.9M (E3; K1), so $30M is 7.69x (K2). That is above the cap, not the 'disciplined 6x' the request describes; the 6.0x only holds on the CIM's $5.0M (E2; K5). The highest price that meets the cap on the same basis is $27.3M (K3), which is $2.7M, or 9%, below the headline (K4, K10). The committee has two options: (a) re-trade to no more than about $27.3M, or to a structure that the board confirms counts as compliant; or (b) take the deal to the full board. This conclusion rests on one unconfirmed premise: that $30M is the amount the mandate measures. No LOI was supplied and the mandate does not define its numerator (E1, E11). Two other things could change the outcome. First, the QoE summary's final status is unconfirmed (E8). Second, single judgments on individual items can move the multiple across 7.0x (E6; K6, K7). Neither is a basis for signing at $30M tomorrow without full-board approval.

## Criteria

### C1 Mandate compliance on QoE EBITDA (weight 35)

Fails. The mandate's denominator is QoE EBITDA (E1). The QoE summary gives $3.9M (E3; K1). At $30M the multiple is 7.69x against a 7.0x cap (K2), so the committee lacks authority to sign at that price. The user's 6.0x uses the CIM's $5.0M (E2; K5), which the mandate expressly does not use. [E1, E2, E3, E4, E9]

*Mechanisms:* The mandate deliberately ties the cap to diligence-adjusted earnings rather than seller-prepared figures, so that the price is measured against earnings an independent provider is prepared to support. The CIM's $1.2M of add-backs come from the sell side. The QoE accepts only $450K of them and adds a -$350K adjustment (E3; K9). That cuts the denominator by $1.1M (K12), which pushes the multiple up from 6.0x to 7.69x.

*Strengths:*
- The mandate text is explicit and VERIFIED (E1).
- The QoE bridge arithmetic is VERIFIED and reproducible (E3; K1).

*Weaknesses:*
- The mandate excerpt is one line with no definitions, so the numerator and any rounding or tolerance convention are unconfirmed (E1).
- The QoE document is a summary whose draft or final status is not stated (E8).

### C2 Validity of each EBITDA add-back and diligence adjustment (weight 20)

Most of the QoE treatments are reasonable, but two rest on thin support. Rejecting the marketing add-back is well supported, because the campaigns recurred in FY2023, FY2024 and FY2025 (E5b). Accepting the legal settlement is asserted ('genuinely non-recurring') with no basis shown (E5c). The owner-compensation add-back is reduced from 600 to 250 on the basis of a ~350 replacement-CEO cost, with no benchmark cited (E5a; K11). The -350 warranty adjustment does not say whether it is an annual under-accrual or a one-off catch-up (E5d). That distinction decides whether it belongs in EBITDA at all (E7). [E2, E3, E5, E6, E7]

*Mechanisms:* Owner compensation can only be added back net of what a replacement will cost, because the owner leaves at close and the role has to be filled (E5a). Spending that recurs every year is an operating cost, not a one-off, so adding it back would overstate run-rate earnings (E5b). If the warranty reserve is under-accrued on an ongoing basis, reported EBITDA overstates true earnings each year, and an EBITDA adjustment is right. If the shortfall is a cumulative catch-up tied to sales made before closing, general practice treats it as a debt-like price deduction instead. The same item should not be counted both ways (E7).

*Strengths:*
- The marketing rejection has explicit three-year evidence (E5b).
- Every adjustment is itemised and reconciles to the reported 3,800 (E3; K1).

*Weaknesses:*
- No compensation benchmarking supports the ~350 CEO cost (E5a).
- There is no settlement documentation (E5c).
- The nature of the warranty item is ambiguous. Even if it is reclassified out of EBITDA, the multiple at a $30M enterprise value is 7.06x, still a breach (E6; K7). Only a non-standard test that nets the item from an equity price while also restoring it to EBITDA gives about 6.98x (K8).
- The guidance on warranty treatment comes from advisory-blog search snippets only (E7).

### C3 Correct numerator: headline price vs enterprise value per mandate definition (weight 15)

Cannot be determined. The mandate does not define the numerator (E1), and no LOI or consideration terms were supplied (E11). So it is unknown whether $30M is enterprise or equity value, whether it is cash-free/debt-free, whether it includes contingent consideration, and what the working-capital peg is. The 7.69x figure assumes $30M is the numerator (E4). [E1, E4, E6, E7, E8, E11]

*Mechanisms:* If $30M is equity value and the target carries debt or debt-like items, enterprise value is above $30M and the breach is worse. Conversely, if $30M is equity value and Tamarind holds net cash, enterprise value is below $30M; roughly $2.7M of net cash (K4) would bring enterprise value down to the $27.3M ceiling (K3). This is a possible path to compliance, but it depends on the same unknown LOI terms (E11). If $30M includes an earn-out and the mandate excludes contingent consideration, the measured price could be lower. Recasting the warranty shortfall as a debt-like item does not help on a conventional enterprise-value test: in a cash-free/debt-free deal a debt-like item reduces the equity cheque, not enterprise value, so $30M against restored EBITDA of $4.25M is still 7.06x (K7). The about 6.98x figure in E6 (K8) only arises if the mandate divides an equity price net of debt-like items by EBITDA. That would be an unusual test mixing equity and enterprise bases, and it must also avoid double counting (E6, E7).

*Strengths:*
- The packet flags the numerator dependency explicitly, so the gap is known rather than hidden (E4, E6).

*Weaknesses:*
- No LOI was supplied (E11).
- The mandate excerpt has no definitions section (E1).
- The QoE summary shows no net debt, net working capital or debt-like analysis (E8).

### C4 Maximum compliant price and renegotiation headroom (weight 15)

On the base case, the ceiling price is $27.3M (K3), a $2.7M (9%) reduction from $30M (K4, K10). The levers, in rough order of how directly they deliver compliance, are as follows. (1) Cut the headline price to $27.3M or less. (2) Defer about $2.7M as an earn-out or contingent consideration. This counts as compliant only if the board or mandate confirms contingent consideration is excluded from the numerator (E1, E11). (3) Use escrow or an indemnity for warranty exposure. This protects value but does not by itself change the measured multiple. A recast of the warranty shortfall as a debt-like deduction is not a reliable compliance lever: on a conventional enterprise-value basis it leaves the multiple at 7.06x, still a breach (K7). It reaches about 6.98x (K8) only under a non-standard equity-price-net-of-debt-like-items test, which would also require the QoE provider to accept that the item is a one-off catch-up (E6, E7). Its main value is as a negotiating argument for a dollar-for-dollar price reduction, which works through lever (1). [E1, E3, E4, E5, E6, E7, E10, E11]

*Mechanisms:* Compliance is price divided by QoE EBITDA. The committee can lower the numerator (price cut, deferral if excluded), or seek board approval above the cap. Moving the warranty item between EBITDA and debt-like items changes the denominator but, under enterprise-value conventions, not the enterprise value, so it does not by itself cure the breach (K7). Reclassification without new evidence would also be second-guessing the QoE provider, which is what the mandate is designed to prevent.

*Strengths:*
- The ceiling and the gap are simple, reproducible numbers (K3, K4).
- General practice treats QoE findings as a normal basis for re-trading price (E10).

*Weaknesses:*
- Whether a structural path (earn-out) counts as compliant depends on mandate definitions that were not supplied (E1, E11).
- The marketing sensitivity (6.98x, K6) would require reversing a well-supported QoE rejection (E5b), so it is not a credible lever.
- The debt-like warranty recast does not deliver compliance on a conventional enterprise-value basis (K7).

### C5 Sustainability and forward risk of the earnings base (weight 10)

QoE EBITDA of $3.9M is a FY2025 point estimate whose run-rate quality cannot be confirmed (E12). There are qualitative downside flags. Marketing that the seller called 'one-time' actually recurs (E5b), which casts doubt on the seller's other characterisations. The warranty reserve was under-accrued (E5d). The replacement-CEO cost is an unbenchmarked estimate (E5a). No TTM trend, warranty claims history or customer-concentration data is available (E12). [E5, E8, E12]

*Mechanisms:* If the replacement CEO costs more than ~350, or warranty under-accrual continues or grows, sustainable EBITDA falls below $3.9M. The effective multiple paid then rises further above 7.0x. This argues for building cushion below the $27.3M ceiling or adding warranty protection, rather than treating $27.3M as a target.

*Strengths:*
- The QoE has already removed the clearest overstatement, the recurring marketing (E3, E5b).

*Weaknesses:*
- There are no data to test run-rate (E12).
- The QoE summary may omit further adjustments (E8).

### C6 LOI and process constraints (weight 5)

Cannot be determined for this LOI, because the LOI was not supplied (E11). General practice is that LOI price terms are usually non-binding and conditioned on diligence, while exclusivity and confidentiality are usually binding, and QoE findings are a common basis for re-trading (E10). That suggests renegotiation is probably available, but it must be checked against the actual LOI before the committee meets. [E10, E11]

*Mechanisms:* If price is non-binding and conditioned on diligence, the buyer can re-open price on the QoE findings without breach. Binding exclusivity limits the seller's alternatives during the window, which supports the buyer's leverage.

*Strengths:*
- Generic practice supports a re-trade (E10).

*Weaknesses:*
- The LOI-practice evidence is search snippets only, and the fetches failed (E10).
- No Tamarind-specific terms are available (E11).

## Alternatives

- **Sign at $30M as proposed:** 7.69x QoE EBITDA (K2) against a 7.0x cap (E1). This is outside delegated authority unless a numerator definition not yet seen in the evidence changes the measure (E11). Not available to the committee on current evidence.
- **Re-trade the headline price to $27.3M or less:** This meets the cap at exactly 7.0x on the base case (K3). It requires a $2.7M (9%) concession from the seller (K4, K10). It is the cleanest route because it does not depend on unconfirmed mandate definitions. Generic LOI practice suggests a QoE-based re-trade is normal (E10).
- **Keep $30M total but make about $2.7M contingent (earn-out or deferred):** Upfront consideration of $27.3M sits at the 7.0x ceiling (K3). This is compliant only if the mandate excludes contingent consideration from the numerator, which is unconfirmed (E1, E11). It also shifts risk onto the seller, which fits the earnings-quality flags (E5, E12).
- **Recast the warranty shortfall as a debt-like deduction:** Not a reliable compliance route. On a conventional enterprise-value basis, enterprise value stays at $30M while EBITDA is restored to $4.25M, giving about 7.06x, still a breach (K7). E6's about 6.98x (K8) requires a non-standard test that divides an equity price net of debt-like items ($29.65M) by EBITDA, mixing two bases, plus QoE agreement that the item is a one-off catch-up (E6, E7). Its practical use is as an argument for a dollar-for-dollar price reduction within the re-trade.
- **Take the deal to the full board at $30M:** This preserves the seller's price. The board must accept 7.69x on QoE EBITDA (K2) and the earnings-quality risks (E5, E12). It is the governance-correct path if the price cannot be moved. The packet contains no synergy or strategic-rationale evidence, so the strongest case the board might weigh cannot be assessed here.

## Tradeoffs

- A price cut to $27.3M secures compliance with certainty, but risks the seller walking. A contingent structure preserves the seller's headline, but its compliance depends on mandate definitions that have not been seen (E1, E11).
- Arguing the warranty shortfall as debt-like does not keep $30M compliant under enterprise-value conventions (still about 7.06x, K7). It is better used to justify part of the price reduction, and pressing it as a compliance device could look like engineering around the QoE provider's conclusion (E6, E7).
- Going to the full board avoids a re-trade, but delays the deal and asks the board to approve above its own cap on a weaker earnings base (E5, E12). Any synergy or strategic case is not in the evidence.

## Edge cases and failure modes

- If $30M is equity value and Tamarind has debt or debt-like items, enterprise value exceeds $30M and the breach is larger than 7.69x (E11, E8). If instead Tamarind holds net cash and $30M is equity value, enterprise value is below $30M; about $2.7M of net cash (K4) would bring it to the $27.3M ceiling (E11).
- If the full QoE report contains further adjustments not shown in the summary (for example NWC or debt-like items), QoE EBITDA or the price basis could move again (E8).
- If the warranty shortfall is both deducted from EBITDA and treated as a debt-like price reduction, value is double-counted (E7).
- If the mandate has an unstated rounding convention, 7.06x (K7) might be argued to round toward 7.1x but not to 7.0x. The 6.98x scenarios (K6, K8) sit within 0.03x of the cap, so a small change in any input flips them (E1, E6).
- If the replacement CEO costs materially more than ~$350K, sustainable EBITDA is lower than $3.9M and even $27.3M implies more than 7.0x on true earnings (E5a, E12).
- If the signed LOI contains a binding price term or a short exclusivity window, the room to re-trade before the committee may be narrower than generic practice suggests (E10, E11).

## Counterarguments

- **The deal is 6.0x adjusted EBITDA, comfortably within a 7x cap.** — The 6.0x uses the banker's CIM figure (E2; K5). The mandate expressly specifies diligence-adjusted (QoE) EBITDA (E1). On that basis the multiple is 7.69x (E3; K2).
- **The QoE is too harsh. Restoring the marketing add-back gets the deal to about 6.98x, inside the cap.** — The marketing campaigns ran in each of FY2023 to FY2025 (E5b). Recurring spend is an operating cost, so restoring the add-back (K6) would override the best-supported QoE judgment.
- **The warranty item is a one-off catch-up and should not reduce run-rate EBITDA.** — Possibly. The QoE summary does not say either way (E5d). But reversing it still gives 7.06x, a breach (K7). Treating it as debt-like instead does not change enterprise value, so on a conventional basis the multiple stays at 7.06x (K7). The about 6.98x figure (K8) needs a non-standard equity-price-net-of-debt-like-items test that the mandate is not shown to use (E1, E6, E7).
- **The $30M may include contingent consideration that the mandate does not count.** — That is possible but unknown, because no LOI was supplied and the mandate has no definitions (E1, E11). The committee should not sign on an assumed favourable definition. It should confirm the definition with the board secretary first.
- **The 0.69x overshoot is small enough for the committee to use judgment.** — The mandate is a hard cap with an explicit route above it: return to the full board (E1). The excerpt states no tolerance (E1).

## Practical implications
