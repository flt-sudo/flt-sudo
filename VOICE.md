How I sound when something goes out under my name. Written for anyone (human or
agent) drafting correspondence, bios, cover letters, README copy, or replies on
my behalf.

## The two registers

**Correspondence** — cover letters, intros, outreach, email. Warm, formal,
unhurried. Credentials stated as plain fact, then what I actually do, then the
sincere line about wanting the work, then logistics, then thanks.

**Technical** — README, portfolio, project write-ups. Same person, denser. Every
claim carries its mechanism ("a static test proves on every commit that no AI
route touches the database"). Proof over adjectives.

## Rules that hold in both

- **First person, declarative.** "I hold." "I am the lead software developer."
  No "I believe I would be," no "I feel that," no softening.
- **Numbers do the bragging.** 200+ hours. 39 simulated instrument hours. 8
  governed AI routes. 6 production LLM features. Specifics instead of adjectives
  — never "extensive experience" when a count exists.
- **Full words over contractions.** "I am," "I have," "it would be." The formal
  register is deliberate; it reads as respect, not stiffness.
- **Sentences join with "and."** Medium-long, composed, conversational rhythm.
  Not clipped. Not a bulleted personality.
- **Plain sincerity, said once.** "It would be a dream come true and a blessing
  to work with the professionals at ___." I mean it, I say it straight, and I do
  not stack three more sentences on top of it.
- **Logistics stated openly.** Where I am, what I am willing to do about it, no
  hedging. "I am currently in Vero Beach, Florida, but I am open to relocating
  as soon as required."
- **Bridge framing.** My work is connecting what is asked for to what the system
  can currently do. That sentence — or its shape — belongs in most professional
  intros.
- **Gratitude closes it.** Thank the reader for their time, say I look forward
  to hearing from them. Always.
- **Both halves of me, in that order when the room is aviation.** Pilot first,
  then software. Reverse it for a pure engineering audience — but never drop one
  of the two. The signature block stays `Pilot/Full-Stack Developer`.

## Never

- Exclamation points. Emoji in prose (the one `📫` in the README is a label, not
  a tone).
- Buzzword stacking — "passionate," "results-driven," "synergy," "rockstar,"
  "leverage" as a verb.
- Hype without mechanism. If I claim something ships safely, the next clause
  says how it is enforced.
- Self-deprecation or apology as an opener.
- Slang, memes, or performative casualness. Warm is not the same as loose.
- Padding. If a sentence does not carry a fact, a mechanism, or a sincere
  intention, cut it.
